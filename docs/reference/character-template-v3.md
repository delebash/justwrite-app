## Part 1 — Identity & Core Engine *(required)*

**Identity:**
- Name / aliases:
- Age:
- Occupation / role:
- Class origin → class now: *(where they started and where they are — the gap between the two is characterization)*
- Education: *(formal or otherwise — this and class are voice inputs: they set vocabulary, assumptions, and what the character notices)*

**In one sentence, this person is:**
> (Not a summary of traits — a summary of tension. "A pacifist who keeps ending up in fights he could have prevented.")

**WANT (external):**
> What they are concretely pursuing. Must be checkable — you can point at the page and say "achieved" or "failed."

**NEED (internal):**
> What would actually make them whole. Usually invisible to them. Often the opposite of the strategy they use to chase the want.

**CORE FEAR:**
> What they organize their life to avoid. Want drives pursuit; fear drives avoidance — scenes need both engines. The fear is usually the lie's shadow.

**THE LIE + ITS ORIGIN:**
> The misbelief driving their bad decisions ("love must be earned," "safety requires control"). Then write the origin as a *scene*, not a summary — where were they, who said what, what did they decide about the world in that moment?

**CENTRAL CONTRADICTION:**
> Two things that are both true about them and don't fit. ("Brutally honest with strangers; lies constantly to the people she loves.") This is where the character stops being a type. If you can't find one, the character isn't done.

**VALUE RANKING UNDER PRESSURE:**
> Pick three values and order them. (e.g., loyalty > truth > safety.) When a scene forces a choice, the higher value wins — until the arc's crisis, when the ranking itself gets tested.

**DECISION HEURISTIC:**
> "Under pressure, they choose ______ over ______." One line. This mechanically generates in-character choices when you're stuck.

**STAKES:**
> What specifically breaks — for them and for others — if they fail to get the want? If nothing breaks, they don't belong in the story.

## Part 2 — Voice & Presence

### Voice
**Register:** (formal ↔ casual; where do they sit, and when do they slip?)
**Rhythm:** (clipped or flowing; do they interrupt; do they trail off?)
**Vocabulary tells:** (jargon, era, region, class markers; 3–5 words only they would use)
**Forbidden words:** (words this character would *never* say — as useful as the tells)
**Subtext habit:** (deflects with jokes; answers questions with questions; agrees then does the opposite)
**Humor style:** (dry / absent / cruel / self-deprecating / none — "no humor" is a choice too)
**Languages:** (what they speak, how well — who can they understand that others can't? This is a plot capability, and it also lives in the ledger.)

**Register shifts by audience:**
| Speaking to… | They become… |
|---|---|
| A superior / authority | |
| Someone with less power | |
| An intimate | |
| A stranger | |
| An enemy | |
> The "less power" row is the reveal — how they treat a waiter, a junior, a servant, a child.

**Three sample lines** (write these — they're the calibration set):
- Calm, ordinary moment: " "

- Lying: " "

### Presence
**How they occupy space:** (posture, gait, stillness or motion, gestures large or contained, coordination — one or two lines that a scene can borrow anytime)
**Presentation:** (what their appearance *choices* signal — fashion, grooming, accessories. Not what they look like; what they're saying with it.)
**Baseline & stress tells:** (their normal physical state, and how being rattled, afraid, or lying leaks through — the tell only reads if the baseline is established)

## Part 3 — Function in the Web

**Thematic argument:** What position does this character embody in the story's central question? (Every major character should answer the theme differently — that's what a cast *is*.)

**Structural relation to the protagonist:** opposition / mirror / temptation / ally / measuring stick — and in what way?

**Perception triad:**
- Self-image: (who they believe they are)
- Public persona: (who they perform being — including any online self)
- Private truth: (who they actually are)
> The gaps between these three are engines. Self-image vs. truth is dramatic irony; persona vs. truth is the mask that slips; self-image vs. persona is the effort of performing.

**Buttons:** (what reliably provokes them — to anger, to shame, to delight. These are levers other characters can pull; every button is a scene waiting to happen.)

**Allegiances & obligations:** (groups, institutions, families, causes they belong to — and what membership *demands* of them. Obligations that conflict with the want are free plot.)

**Relationship dynamics** (dynamics, not lists):
| Person | Wants from them | Fears from them | Speaks to them like… |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |
| | | | |

**How they escalate conflict:** (aggression, withdrawal, sabotage, weaponized competence, going over heads…)

**Cornered behavior:** What do they do when they have no good options? This is the moment readers learn who they really are.

## Part 4 — Capabilities & Limits *(genre module — skip for realist fiction)*

**Abilities:** (magic, tech, training, powers — what can they actually do?)
**Costs:** (what each use takes from them — energy, sanity, time, moral weight, resources)
**Hard limits:** (what they cannot do, ever. Limits generate more plot than powers; readers track limits, not strengths.)
**Conditions:** (what the ability requires — tools, materials, states, permissions)
**Who knows:** (who is aware of these capabilities — this row feeds the knowledge-state ledger below)

## Part 5 — Continuity Ledger *(facts that must never drift)*

**Physical constants:** Only features that appear on the page. (Scar on left hand, limp in cold weather, the tattoo with a story.) If it's never mentioned, it doesn't go here. Marks with origin stories do double duty — note the origin.

**Conditions & disabilities:** (physical or mental — anything that changes what's possible in a scene: mobility, stamina, perception, medication, triggers. These are scene-logistics facts, not traits.)

**Languages:** (repeated here from voice, because "who can read the letter / overhear the plan" is a continuity fact)

**Timeline anchors:** Birth year, and dates of any event referenced in-story. Do the math once, write it down, never re-derive it.

**Knowledge state at story start:**
- Knows:
- Doesn't know:
- Believes wrongly:
> (This is the single most common source of continuity errors and POV leaks — a character acting on information they haven't received yet.)

**Secrets:**

**Possessions with story weight:** (Objects that will matter — including attachment objects: the car, the ring, the dead brother's watch. Note where each one is.)

## Part 6 — Arc as Belief Revision

Frame the arc as evidence for and against the lie, not as plot slots.

1. **Belief at start:** The lie, operationalized as behavior.
2. **Confirming events:** Early story moments that *seem* to prove the lie true (the world cooperates with their dysfunction).
3. **First crack:** The moment the lie fails to explain something.
4. **Crisis choice:** The scene where lie and truth demand opposite actions and the character must pick. This should force a violation of the Part 1 value ranking.
5. **End state:** Adopted the truth / doubled down on the lie (negative arc) / never needed to change and instead changed the world (flat arc).

## Part 8 — Prompt Card *(distilled, ~200–300 tokens)*

The compact block for injecting into an AI generation prompt — or for your own quick reference. Everything above compresses into this:

```
CHARACTER: [Name], [age] — [occupation/role]
CORE: Wants [want]. Needs [need]. Fears [fear]. Believes [lie].
CONTRADICTION: [the two clashing truths]
VALUES: [A] > [B] > [C]. Under pressure chooses [X] over [Y].
BUTTONS: provoked to anger by [x]; to shame by [y].
VOICE: [register], [rhythm]. Says: [tell words]. Never says: [forbidden]. Habit: [subtext habit].
PRESENCE: [one line — how they move]. Stress tell: [tell].
TO [key person]: speaks [how]. TO [key person]: speaks [how].
KNOWS: [critical knowledge]. DOESN'T KNOW: [gaps]. WRONGLY BELIEVES: [errors].
LIMITS: [capabilities + hard limits — genre only]
CONSTANTS: [physical/timeline/language facts that must not drift]
NOW: [current scene goal — fill in per scene]
```

**Card discipline:** the card has grown — trim per character; not every line earns its tokens for every character. Update KNOWS / DOESN'T KNOW as the story advances; swap NOW every scene. The card is the character's working memory — the dossier above is yours.
