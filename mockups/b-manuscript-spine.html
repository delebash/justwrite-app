scene 2 · the low tide

The wood was warmer than she'd expected, and the ink had taken. Iris held the folio above the lapping water for a moment longer than she needed to, watching her own breath cloud against the inside of the leather. It had been her mother's, of course; she knew that now in the way you knew which hand of yours was the left one.

Behind her, the harbourmaster was saying something about the equinox — about the equinox and how the channel ran shallow at the far rocks for an hour either side of noon — but she wasn't listening. She was thinking instead about Theodore. About what he would have done, finding a folio half-buried at low tide. He would not have held it carefully. He would have opened it on the sand.

She did not open it on the sand.
