The Cartographer's Daughter

Chapter Twelve

The First Crossing

She pressed her thumb to the vellum where the coastline should have been, and felt only the cold weave of the cloth, and the small, terrible certainty that her father had drawn the sea from memory. The chart was beautiful. That was the worst of it — the soundings inked in his careful hand, the rhumb lines fanning from a rose that pointed, she now understood, at nothing.

Above her, the deck complained in its joints. @Wren would be at the wheel by now, pretending the fog was weather and not an omen, and she would have to tell him before the watch changed that the harbour he steered for had never been surveyed by anyone living.

She rolled the chart and did not roll it tightly, the way her father had taught her — a map remembers every crease, and a crease becomes a road — and she wondered how many roads she had folded into the world without meaning to.

✦ ✦ ✦

Below deck the lantern threw its yellow argument against the dark. She spread the second chart, the one she had not shown him, and weighted its corners with the brass dividers and a cup gone cold. Here the coast existed. Here it had teeth.

The difference between the two maps was three days' sailing and, she suspected, both their lives.
